Yes. That distinction is probably the hinge:

**possession can evidence a grant without constituting the grant.**

That keeps the Non-Conversion Law from accidentally outlawing legitimate authority.

Ownership, office, contract, custody, credentials, keys, records, technical permissions — all of those can be **traces, instruments, or consequences** of standing. But if the doctrine starts treating the held thing itself as the source, it recreates the exact failure it is supposed to prohibit.

So the stress-test question becomes:

> **If the possession vanished but the underlying grant remained, would the standing still exist?**

If yes, the standing came from the grant.

And the inverse:

> **If the possession remained but the underlying grant expired, was revoked, exceeded scope, or never existed, would standing remain?**

If yes, the doctrine is leaking possession into legitimacy.

That gives the candidate a very clean test pair.

And the positive rule now has a stronger form:

> **Information may cross seats. Authority may cross seats only through an independently valid grant.**

That is probably the real boundary rule underneath the whole thing.

Because you do need movement. Evidence has to inform judgment. Judgment has to inform action. Legitimacy has to authorize some actions. But none of those transitions should happen merely because one seat already holds something adjacent.

So the distinction is no longer just:

**influence flows, authority doesn’t.**

It is:

**influence may flow by relevance; authority may flow only by grant.**

That feels like the sentence the seven pressures should now try to kill.
