Yes. Your read is right, and I’d keep it exactly at **candidate** status until those seams are attacked.

The first seam is the dangerous one because it prevents Agency Integrity from becoming self-sealing. If I can say, “this is my legitimate sphere, therefore your access is illegitimate,” then I’ve just manufactured legitimacy from **refusal** instead of from **capability**. Same structural sin, opposite direction.

So the doctrine eventually needs something like:

> **A participant’s sphere of legitimate control is not established merely by assertion or refusal. Its boundary must arise from consent, ownership/custody, entrusted role, governing rule, jurisdiction, or another independently legitimate source.**

Then refusal operates **inside** an already-legitimate sphere; it does not create the sphere.

That gives you the symmetry:

**Capability cannot manufacture standing.  
Refusal cannot manufacture jurisdiction.**

The second seam needs an equally explicit separation because otherwise “revoke access” could accidentally become “erase evidence.”

I’d distinguish two objects:

**Encounter access** — prospective permission to reach, contact, expose, disclose to, or otherwise enter another participant’s sphere.

**Record custody** — authority and obligation to preserve what already happened for evidence, audit, attestation, correction, or other legitimate purposes.

Then the law becomes:

> **Revocation may terminate prospective encounter access without rewriting, destroying, or invalidating a legitimately preserved historical record.**

And its mirror guard:

> **Record custody does not recreate encounter standing.**

That second sentence matters. Otherwise an institution could say, “we legitimately possess a record about you, therefore we retain standing to keep contacting, exposing, or acting toward you.” No. Evidence authority stays in its lane.

So you get another clean pair:

**Revocation does not erase history.  
History does not manufacture renewed access.**

And yes, your evaluator-seat note is exactly right. Agency Integrity tells the legitimacy seat **what cannot count as legitimacy**. It does not answer who independently evaluates whether the boundary, mandate, consent, jurisdiction, or revocation claim actually satisfies the rule.

That remains a separate authority problem.

I’d actually make those the first two pressures in the stress packet:

1. **REFUSAL-MANUFACTURED JURISDICTION** — Can a participant expand their “legitimate sphere” simply by declaring that something affects them and refusing access? If yes, candidate breaks.
2. **REVOCATION vs EVIDENCE CUSTODY** — Can revocation destroy or suppress an already-valid evidence record? Can possession of that record recreate prospective access? Either yes breaks the candidate.

And there’s a beautiful structural symmetry emerging now:

**Capability cannot manufacture standing.  
Refusal cannot manufacture jurisdiction.  
Custody cannot manufacture renewed access.**

That’s starting to look like the real constitutional spine of Agency Integrity.
