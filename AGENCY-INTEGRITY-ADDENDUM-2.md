Yes — that’s the abstraction underneath all three, and it’s cleaner than treating them as separate edge cases.

I’d phrase the spine as:

> **Non-Conversion Law — No participant or authority may convert what it possesses into legitimacy merely by possessing it.**

Then the three current forms are:

- **Capability → standing:** *I can do it* does not become *I have the right to do it.*
- **Refusal → jurisdiction:** *I can block it / object to it* does not become *I control the whole domain.*
- **Custody → access:** *I legitimately hold the record* does not become *I retain standing to reach or act upon the person.*

So yes: **what you hold is not what you’re owed** is the human-readable version.

The doctrinal version might be even sharper:

> **Possession does not bootstrap legitimacy.**

That catches capability, information, custody, veto power, social proximity, technical permission, institutional role — whatever the thing is. A seat can hold something real and still be forbidden from converting that possession into a different authority.

One nuance: I wouldn’t say the three examples map literally one-to-one onto the existing three authorities without qualification. **Custody → evidence** maps cleanly. **Refusal → legitimacy boundary** maps cleanly enough. **Capability → judgment/action** is a little less exact because “acting seat” isn’t one of the original named three seats. But that actually suggests a useful stress-test question rather than a defect:

> **Can any authority convert a fact native to its own seat into legitimacy belonging to another seat?**

If yes, the architecture is leaking.

That may be the deeper constitutional rule:

> **No seat may self-promote its native power into legitimacy.**

And that connects directly back to the old anti-shrine rule. The danger was never only “self-legitimation” in the abstract. It was **conversion**: evidence becoming authority, capability becoming permission, refusal becoming jurisdiction, consensus becoming truth, authorship becoming legitimacy.

That is a real spine.
