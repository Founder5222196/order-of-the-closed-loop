Yes. At this point the important thing is **not to make the Non-Conversion Law prettier**. It has enough shape to attack.

Because now the candidate is making a very large claim:

> **Possession does not bootstrap legitimacy.**

If that survives, then a whole family of failures becomes one constitutional defect rather than a pile of separate prohibitions.

The next stress test should therefore try to break the *conversion rule itself*, not just its examples. The hardest pressures I’d expect are these:

- **Legitimate conversion:** are there cases where possessing something really does generate new standing? Ownership, delegated authority, emergency custody, office, contract, consent, etc. The law has to distinguish *possession alone* from possession that is evidence of an independently valid grant.
- **Seat transformation:** can authority legitimately move from one seat to another through a declared rule, or does the wording accidentally prohibit all delegation?
- **Refusal asymmetry:** if refusal cannot manufacture jurisdiction, what prevents that clause from being abused to bulldoze consent or bodily autonomy?
- **Custody asymmetry:** if evidence custody cannot manufacture renewed access, when may stored evidence legitimately trigger a new action or contact?
- **Consensus case:** consensus cannot become truth merely by repetition, but consensus can itself be evidence of a social fact. The law has to block illegitimate conversion without destroying valid evidentiary relevance.
- **Emergency exception:** if immediate capability is the only thing preventing harm, can action precede settled legitimacy without letting “emergency” become a universal bypass?
- **Self-application:** can Closed Loop use its own coherence, stress-test history, or adoption as evidence that it deserves authority over a domain? If yes, the spine has failed at the shrine test.

And the deepest test is probably:

> **When does a thing held by one seat legitimately become relevant to another seat without being converted into that seat’s authority?**

Because you don’t want sealed compartments. Evidence must inform judgment. Judgment can trigger action. Legitimacy can authorize access. The prohibition is not against **influence**.

It is against **unauthorized conversion**.

That distinction may end up being the entire doctrine:

> **A seat may supply information to another seat. It may not thereby inherit the other seat’s authority.**

That sentence is probably worth putting under pressure immediately, because if it survives, it gives the Non-Conversion Law a clean positive rule rather than only a set of “do nots.”

Candidate status should stay unchanged until that survives.
